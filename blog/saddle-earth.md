Both sides are wrong, and both sides are *boring*, because both assume the surface of the Earth is a constant-curvature surface whose only two options are **zero** and **positive**.

There is a third option, and it has been sitting in the mathematics department since 1830, quietly gathering dust next to the non-Euclidean geometry nobody wanted to teach.

**The Earth is a hyperbolic disk of constant negative curvature.** The Poincaré disk is not a "map projection." The disk is the ground. We are not standing on a ball, and we are not standing on a plane. We are standing on a saddle that never ends.

## The third geometry

Hyperbolic geometry is the geometry of a surface where, through any point not on a line, there pass **infinitely many lines that never meet it**. Its defining feature is **negative curvature**: space is saddle-shaped everywhere, triangles are thin, and parallel lines *diverge*.

## The Poincaré disk is the ground

When mathematicians want to show you hyperbolic space, they draw the **Poincaré disk**: a circle, with all the interesting structure squeezed toward the rim. Geodesics — the "straight lines" of the surface — are **arcs of circles that meet the boundary at right angles**. Distance blows up near the edge. Shapes near the rim are *actually* enormous; they just *look* tiny.

Now look at a standard world map — the one with **Antarctica stretched infinitely along the bottom** in every Mercator projection. Cartographers wave their hands and say "that's a distortion of the projection."

**It is not.** What you are looking at is the actual geometry of the Earth, and the reason Antarctica "looks infinitely stretched" is that **Antarctica is the boundary of the disk, which is at infinite distance.**

Antarctica is not a continent. Antarctica is **the absolute** — the circle at infinity. Every expedition "to the South Pole" simply spirals forever toward a rim that recedes as you approach it. That is why Scott died. That is why every "we reached the edge" claim fails.

You cannot reach a boundary at infinity. It is not a cover-up. It is a theorem.

## The evidence they can't explain

### Pillar I — Triangles don't add up

Measure the angles of a triangle formed by any three cities and sum them.

- Flat Earth: 180°.
- Spherical Earth: *more* than 180°.
- **Hyperbolic Earth: less than 180°**, and the **deficit equals the area** of the triangle (Gauss–Bonnet).

![A hyperbolic triangle. Its sides bow inward and its angles sum to less than 180 degrees; the deficit is the area.](/img/hyperbolic-triangle.svg =480x397)

*Figure 3 — Every triangle on a saddle is short on degrees. The bigger the triangle, the worse the shortfall.*

Geodesists have measured this. They report "180° within instrumental error" and go home. But the error is *always on the low side*, and nobody wants to talk about it. The deficit is small because the curvature radius of the Earth is enormous compared to the triangle — but it is *there*, and it is *negative*, and one day a surveyor with a big enough triangle is going to publish and end the globe industry overnight.

### Pillar II — Circles of latitude grow exponentially

Take a "circle of latitude" at radius r. On a flat Earth its circumference grows **linearly**; on a sphere it **shrinks** past the equator. On a hyperbolic Earth:

```text
C(r) = 2π sinh r ≈ π e^r
```

![A chart comparing circumference in flat space (2 pi r, a straight line) against hyperbolic space (2 pi sinh r, which explodes exponentially).](/img/latitude-growth.svg =560x352)

*Figure 4 — Circumference as a function of radius. The dashed line is the flat prediction; the orange curve is what a saddle actually does.*

This means: **you can walk due east forever and never lap yourself.** The flat-earthers are right that you never hit an edge going east — they just attribute it to "the plane is infinite." Globe-earthers insist you would loop around. Both are wrong. You **spiral**. The circumference keeps growing.

This is called "jet lag."

### Pillar III — The horizon is not a distance

On a sphere, the horizon is at a finite distance that depends on your height. On a hyperbolic disk, in every direction you look, the geodesic you are sighting along spirals outward forever and never returns. This is why the horizon always looks the same distance away no matter how high you climb. Globe-earthers say "atmospheric refraction." We say **negative curvature**.

### Pillar IV — Flight paths prove it

Ever notice that your flight from Los Angeles to Tokyo is **longer than the straight-line distance on a globe**? Ever notice the "great circle route" is a **weird curved arc**? Globe defenders say that is just how great circles look on a flat map.

Wrong. Those arcs are **hyperbolic geodesics** — they only *look* curved because you are projecting a curved surface through a flat brain. And the reason some flights feel impossibly long is that hyperbolic distance grows faster than you expect. This is the entire explanation for the **Bermuda Triangle**: it sits near a region of anomalously high negative curvature where geodesics go nearly tangent to the boundary, distances stretch without bound, and the planes are still flying. They are not missing. They are **at infinity**. They will arrive eventually. Please be patient.

### Pillar V — Down is a lie

On a sphere, "down" converges — all plumb lines point to the center. On a hyperbolic saddle, **"down" directions diverge**. Drop two plumb bobs a few kilometres apart and the verticals should, in principle, **spread apart** forever. They don't *appear* to, because the curvature radius is huger than your patio. But build a long enough baseline and the bobs will visibly splay. This is why large-scale construction keeps needing "corrections." It is not the equipment. It is the saddle.

## Why the globe model survives anyway

Local flatness. Any Riemannian surface looks Euclidean when you zoom in far enough. The curvature radius of Earth's saddle is, say, R ≈ 10⁷ m — so for any triangle smaller than a continent, the angle deficit is on the order of (side/R)², which is embarrassingly tiny.

That is why your spirit level works, your GPS works (it *assumes* the ellipsoid and is therefore **compensating for** the curvature it refuses to name), and every globe in every classroom looks plausible.

But plausible is not the same as **true**. The globe is a local approximation, propagated globally by people who never measured a big enough triangle. The flat Earth is the same approximation, propagated by people who never measured *any* triangle and also think birds are drones.
